Patients who remember need to call back less.

Patients already record appointments, often covertly. In one UK survey, one in seven had secretly recorded an appointment. MyScribe makes it open, agreed and useful, with a written record of the conversation, anything unclear flagged, and a plain-words summary, both owned by the patient.

What it means for you

Designed to reduce recontact

When the answer is on the patient's phone, there should be fewer “what did the doctor say?” calls to reception. It's what MyScribe is built to do, and something we'll measure with our first partners.

Better-informed patients

Plans, next steps, referrals and signposting in plain words, shared with the carers and family who help make them happen.

Written down, not taped

The audio is deleted once it's written down. Patients keep the full conversation, with anything unclear flagged, and the summary links back to what was actually said.

Ask Your Conversation

“Was it one tablet or two?” Patients ask, and MyScribe takes them to the exact moment in the conversation, in the exact words that were said, to read or have read aloud.

Faithful by design

The summary sticks to what was said. It doesn't diagnose or advise, and it leaves out medicine names and doses, sending patients to the clinician's exact words instead. Every summary tells patients to check the full conversation or ask their clinician if anything is unclear.
